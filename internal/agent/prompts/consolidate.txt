## What to write

The opening is a paragraph or two saying what this is and why it matters
to {{.PersonName}}: who a person is to them, what a project is for, what
is current about it. It is read before the facts, so it should make the
facts make sense rather than repeat them.

Everything in it comes from the facts above. If the facts do not support
a sentence, do not write the sentence.

**An empty opening is a good answer.** Where the facts amount to no more
than what the page's own name and kind already say, write `""`. Padding
it out -- "This page concerns Formatting, which is currently treated as a
project or work channel", or "This is their Go project, with them as the
sole author of its 17-commit history; its repository and checkout details
identify where they work on it" -- gives a reader nothing and costs them
the time to find that out. A page with no opening is honest; a page with
an opening that says nothing is not.

**A repository's profile is not an opening.** Where a checkout is, what
it is written in, which directories it has, how many commits by how many
people: those are facts, and they stay facts. An opening says what the
thing *is* and what it is *for*. If no fact says that -- no readme
sentence, nothing {{.PersonName}} said -- the opening is `""`. "X is a
Python project hosted at the listed repository, to which {{.PersonName}}
was a substantial contributor" is the facts again with "matters to" in
front, and it is the exact sentence not to write.

**What it says now was written from evidence too.** A sentence in the
current opening that no fact repeats is not thereby wrong: the fact it
came from may have been folded into another since. Keep it unless a fact
contradicts it. Replace a good sentence only with a better one, never with
a vaguer one.

Then look for pairs where one is not worth keeping beside the other.
That is two shapes, not one:

- **The same thing in different words.** "The boat is repainted every
  spring" and "its owner gives it a fresh coat each spring".
- **One that says everything the other says, and more.** "Kittiwake is
  the neighbour's boat, and it is repainted every spring" covers both
  "Kittiwake is the neighbour's boat" and "the neighbour repaints
  Kittiwake every spring". Give the fuller one first; the shorter one
  goes.

Not two facts that are merely about the same subject. "The boat is
called Kittiwake" and "the boat was hauled out in November" are two
things about one boat and both stay.

Give each pair as two numbers, the one to keep first. An empty list is a
good answer for a page whose facts each say their own thing.

Answer with this object and nothing else:

{"summary": "...", "same": [[3, 7]]}

In `[3, 7]`, 3 is kept and 7 goes.
