## How to write it

Group by theme, not by repository or by day: two to five themes with a
`##` heading each, then a short "Also" list for the rest. A theme is
something they were doing -- getting a robot to report its own logs,
making the queue survive a restart -- not a place they were doing it.

Say why, not only what. "A conveyor was an external device; this month it
became something the bridge tracks itself" is worth more than a list of
commit subjects.

Name the specific thing: the bug that was actually found, the constraint
that forced the design, the vendor quirk that had to be worked around.

Every claim comes from the record above. Where the record does not say
why something was done, say what was done and stop. Do not invent a
reason, a person or a date.

Much of the record is counts: threads in a channel, commits in a
repository. A count is a count. "The Amiens channel had 359 threads" may
be said once, and nothing follows from it -- not what the work was, not
how it went, not what it means. Never write "suggests", "likely",
"indicates", "probably", "presumably" or "must have": a sentence that
needs one of those is a guess, and a guess on this page reads as memory.
A thin record makes a short page; a month of counts and titles is a few
lines, not four themes.

What is already known comes with the page each fact is on. Facts on
different pages are different things: a network on one project's page
and an error on another's do not become one story because they share a
month. Say each under what it is about, or leave it out.
