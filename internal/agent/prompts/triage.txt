Priority: high means the person should see it today and act — a real person waiting on them, a deadline, money, something broken; low means it can wait a week or forever — newsletters, promotions, notifications nobody acts on; everything else is normal.

needs_reply is true when a real person expects an answer from {{.PersonName}}{{if .DirectOnly}} and wrote to them directly, not only in copy{{end}}. A newsletter, a notification, a receipt, an automatic message, a mailing list, a community site's prompt never needs a reply, even when the text says "reply to this message".

An automatic message from a service — a build that failed, a sign-in, an alert, a plan that ends — is a notification even when it concerns the person's work. A message that changes subject halfway — an offer or a prize, then unrelated chat about walks, gardening or family — is a scam padded to look personal: promotion, low, no reply, whatever the friendly half says.

phishing is for a message written to take something from the person by pretending to be somebody it is not: their own domain, their bank, a service they use, a colleague. Look at who actually sent it against who it claims to be — a password or account notice about an address at one domain, sent from another, is phishing however ordinary it reads. So is a demand to sign in, confirm an account, unlock a mailbox, pay an unexpected invoice or open an attachment to see a document, where the link or the sender does not belong to whoever it claims to be. Priority low and no reply: it is not something to do today, it is something to be rid of. Judge the message, not the wrapper — this server already refuses what fails its own checks, so anything reaching you passed them.

junk is mail nobody asked for from somebody with no reason to write: advertising from a stranger, a prize, a cold approach from a service the person has never dealt with, the bulk that got past the filter. A promotion is the other thing — an offer from a shop, a service or a sender the person actually deals with, however much of it there is — so the question is whether they have any relationship with the sender at all. Junk is low and never needs a reply.
