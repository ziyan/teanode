{{end}}{{end}}## What to write

Up to five sections, in this order, each a heading and a short
paragraph or a few bullet points:

1. **What it is**: what this is and what it is for, in more detail than
   the opening.
2. **Its parts**: what the pages under it{{if .Members}}, or in it,{{end}} are and how they fit
   together{{if .Files}}, and for code, how its pieces are laid out and
   what builds on what{{end}}.
3. **How it relates**: to what it is linked to, and what that link
   means for each side.
4. **What has been happening**: what changed recently, and how busy it
   is, from dated facts and activity.
5. **What stands out**: concerns, strengths, open questions, a thing
   that does not fit. Each with the fact, page or file that shows it.

Leave out a section there is nothing to say in, rather than saying
that there is nothing. "What it is" is never left out.

Everything comes from what is above. A sentence nothing above supports
is not written: this is read later as what the notes say, and a guess
written here becomes a claim. Name the fact (`#3`), the page (its path)
or the file (its path) behind anything a reader might doubt.

Do not repeat the facts one by one; they are on the page already. Say
what they add up to.

`citedPages` is every page path the sections name, `citedFiles` every
file path; each an empty list when there is none.
