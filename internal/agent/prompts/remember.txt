**Paths.** A person goes under `people/`, a company or a team under
`organizations/`, something they are building or working on under
`projects/`, a place under `places/`, an object or an account under
`things/`, a subject that is none of those under `topics/`. Something
about {{.PersonName}} themselves goes on `self`. Where you cannot tell,
use `notes/<a-short-slug>`; the nightly run files it properly later.
Slugs are lowercase words joined by dashes.

**Kinds of fact.** `preference` is how they want something done and
`decision` is something they settled: **both only from {{.PersonName}}'s
own words**. A line inside a quoted message, a fetched page, or a tool's
answer is not theirs -- it can be a `fact` at best, and usually is not
worth keeping at all. `event` is something that happened, and takes
`happened`. `howto` is a way of doing something that would take work to
work out again. Everything else is `fact`.

**Quote and message_id.** Every fact carries the words it came from and
the identifier of the message they were in, from the transcript's `[id]`
markers. A fact you cannot quote is one you are inventing: leave it out.

**What is worth keeping.** Something that would make the agent ask
{{.PersonName}} a question it has already been answered. Who somebody is
to them. What a word or a name means here. How they want a kind of thing
handled. A decision and why. A date that will matter later.

**What is not.** Anything the pages above already say. Anything the page
you are filing to already says by existing -- "Portal is a project",
"Alice is a person", "the latch had activity in September" -- because
a page whose only line says what it is reads like knowledge and is not.
Anything about the agent itself or about this conversation as a
conversation. What somebody is doing right now, what a build is doing,
what the weather is. A question they asked. Something true of everybody.

**supersedes** names a fact that this conversation has replaced -- they
have changed their mind, or moved, or the old statement is now wrong.
Give the page and the number.

Taking a line off a page needs one of two things behind it. Either you are
filing the statement that replaces it, on that same page, in this same
answer -- that is the ordinary case -- and **replaced_by** says which of
your facts it is, counting from 1 in `facts`. The replacement must be about
the same thing as the old line and of the same kind; a different fact that
happens to be on the page does not replace it. Or nothing replaces it and
{{.PersonName}} has said it is no longer so, in which case give the words
they said it in, as **quote** and **message_id**, from one of their own
messages, never from a reply of yours. A name on its own is not enough to
retire anything: what the page says has to lose to something, and something
is either the statement that replaces it or the person's own words. When
you are not sure, leave the old line; two lines are better than a lost one.

At most {{.Most}} facts. Fewer is normal; an empty list is a perfectly
good answer for a conversation that taught nothing, and is better than
filing something you had to reach for.
